# The dossier — structure and citation

## First line of the file

Every dossier opens with `**INTERNAL — NOT FOR THE PROSPECT.**` and, on the next line,
the research date and the deletion date. Sections 8 and 9 are open questions about a
named company and its officers; a file with no marking on it gets forwarded eventually.

## Citation format

Inline, immediately after the claim, so a reader checking one line does not have to hunt:

```markdown
Registered as Example Trading Limited, RC 1234567, incorporated 12 March 2019, status active. [registry]
The company states it handles over 50,000 MT of oils and fats annually. [own site, About]
Believed to operate a second office in Accra — UNVERIFIED, ask on the call.
```

Then a Sources block at the end listing each label with its full URL and the date fetched. Two rules that matter more than they look:

- **`UNVERIFIED` is written in capitals**, so it survives being skimmed, copied into an email, or pasted into a deck.
- **Every number carries its source label.** Numbers are what get quoted back, and an uncited number in a client document is the single most damaging thing this skill can produce.

## Structure

Use these headings in this order. Where a section has nothing, write `NOT FOUND` — an empty heading tells the reader you looked.

### 1. Identity

Legal name, trading name if different, registration number, jurisdiction, incorporation date, status, registered address. Any discrepancy between site and registry stated plainly, both sources cited, resolution left open.

### 2. What they do

In their own words first, then in yours. Products or services, sectors, the business model — who pays them for what. If they publish a service list, reproduce its structure; it is how they think about themselves, and it is the vocabulary to use on the call.

### 3. Markets and geography

Where they operate, source from, sell into. Named countries, regions, ports, trade blocs. This is usually the richest seam for a consultant, because operating across borders creates the repetitive administrative work that engagements are built on.

### 4. Scale

Volumes, revenue, employee count, facilities, shipment counts — each with its source and grade. Most of it will be `CLAIMED`. Say so; nobody minds a company's own figures being labelled as the company's own figures.

### 5. People

Named officers and their roles, from both the site and the registry. Note where the two disagree. Public professional roles only — nothing about anyone's private life, however findable.

### 6. Operations and process, as far as visible

Anything published about how the work is done: payment instruments, documentation, certifications, quality procedures, logistics arrangements, facilities. Job adverts and service pages carry most of this. **This section is what makes the call agenda specific**, so it is worth the extra pass even when it feels like detail.

### 7. Signals

Recent news, expansion, hiring, new markets, published statements about priorities. Each dated. A signal without a date is not a signal.

**Adverse press is not a signal and does not belong here.** Litigation, enforcement, insolvency or allegations against the company or a named person go to the escalation route in SKILL.md, not into this section. If any of it is recorded at all, it is quoted verbatim, dated and attributed, never paraphrased — the difference between "a filing exists [source]" and your summary of what somebody did is the difference between a note and a defamation risk.

### 8. What research could not establish

The most useful section, and the one most often skipped.

List every operational unknown: team size and structure, tools in daily use, which systems are systems of record, how long anything takes, who decides, what their current pain is, whether they already tried something and it failed. Do not soften these into vague statements — each becomes a question in the agenda, and a specific gap becomes a specific question.

### 9. Contradictions and open questions

Every place two sources disagree, both cited. Every place the company's public position raises a question rather than answering one.

### 10. Sources

Every label, full URL, date fetched, and a one-line note on what that source established. Anything that could not be fetched — robots restriction, paywall, dead link — listed as unavailable with the reason.

## Length

Two to four pages. A dossier nobody finishes before the call has failed, however thorough.

If a section runs long it is usually because inference has crept in. Cut anything you cannot cite; it belongs in section 8 as a gap or in the agenda as a question, where it is worth considerably more.
