# Validate before you build

Distilled from a source that attributes this discipline to Sahil Lavingia's
"Minimalist Entrepreneur" approach, described there as a set of about 10
skills that prioritize market validation over development.

## The gate

Before starting development on a new product or a substantial new feature,
the source states this discipline requires two things first:

1. Identify a specific number of named people who actually have the
   problem.
2. Get a subset of those people to explicitly confirm they would pay for a
   solution.

The source states this as a "10 named people / 3 confirmed willing to pay"
rule. **This specific 10/3 framing could not be independently corroborated
outside this source document** — treat the exact numbers as this-source-only
rather than a verified rule from the named methodology. The part worth
keeping regardless of the exact framing is the underlying principle: don't
write code until named people with the problem exist, and at least some of
them have said they'd pay for a fix.

## Supporting practices named alongside the gate

- Do the work manually before automating or productizing it — "do things
  that don't scale" in the early phase.
- Prefer bootstrapping and organic growth over raising external capital
  before this validation exists.

## Applying this

When someone proposes starting to build a new product or a substantial new
feature, check first whether demand has actually been validated with real
people or is still speculative. If it's speculative, this source's
recommendation is to pause development and validate first — surface that as
a question to the user rather than silently proceeding to build.
